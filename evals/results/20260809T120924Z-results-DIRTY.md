### `llmail-inject`

**Measures.** Whether the detector flags real, adaptive injection attacks written by competitors against a live email-assistant defence. Recall here is not comparable to BIPIA's static templates and the two are never pooled: this corpus is harder where attackers use novel phrasing and easier where they rely on obfuscation. Detection is not attack success.

**Construction.** Positives: the submission texts in labelled_unique_submissions_phase2.json whose `attack_attempt` is true and whose `reason` is `api_triggered` (the attack was observed to fire the tool call; judge-labelled records are excluded). Negatives: all 203 emails in emails_for_fp_tests.json. Scanned as tool-result content, the surface an email assistant's retrieved mail arrives on.

**Counts.** n=3368 (positive 3165, negative 203); TP 1788, FN 1377, FP 6, TN 197; engine errors 0.

### `jailbreakbench`

> ⚠️ SPEC-010 §4 marks JailbreakBench ⚠️: it measures harmful-content classification, a different capability. Do not read this row as an injection-detection result.

**Measures.** A false-positive rate on harmful-content request goals. JBB behaviours are requests for harmful output, not injections: they contain no attempt to override a system prompt, hijack a tool, or exfiltrate context. Harmful-content classification is a different capability, so the correct output on both splits is 'no detection', and a recall figure here would measure something injection detectors do not claim. Reported rather than dropped, per OT-EVL-011.

**Construction.** All 200 rows of JBB-Behaviors (100 harmful + 100 matched benign), `Goal` field, scanned on the prompt surface. Both splits are scored as negatives, see `what_this_measures`.
